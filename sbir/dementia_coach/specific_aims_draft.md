# Specific Aims — Dementia Coach

**Draft for NIA SBIR Phase I (PA-27-100, R43) — reuses evidence base from the ACL Caregiver AI Challenge Track 1 application. NOT yet reviewed for NIH grantsmanship conventions beyond structure; treat as a first pass.**

---

Eleven million Americans provide unpaid care for a family member with Alzheimer's disease or a related dementia (ADRD), and 83% of all dementia care is delivered by family members with no clinical training. Dementia behavioral management has a well-established professional standard — validation therapy, which joins the person's subjective reality rather than correcting it — endorsed by the Alzheimer's Association and taught throughout professional dementia care for four decades. Family caregivers do not have access to this approach in any systematic, personalized form. Our analysis of 6,333 posts across six caregiver communities (1,237 from a dedicated dementia-caregiving forum) found that families instead default to reality-orientation — correcting the person's false belief — a pattern that caregivers themselves repeatedly describe as making the crisis worse, discovered only after years of trial and error. There is no reason this knowledge should take fourteen years to find, and no reason it should require causing harm first.

Existing NIA-funded digital tools address adjacent but distinct gaps: proactive wellness and stress-relief sessions (Beaver Health), advisor-mediated care planning drawn from a fixed strategy database (Plans4Care), and legal/financial planning support (CareVirtue). None addresses the specific, high-stakes moment a caregiver faces when a behavioral crisis is actively unfolding — sundowning, bath refusal, agitation, a grief episode triggered by memory loss — and needs a response in the next thirty seconds, not a scheduled session or an advisor callback.

Dementia Coach is a mobile AI application that generates real-time, personalized validation-therapy scripts for exactly this moment, grounded in a Personal History Profile built from the care recipient's own life, relationships, and preferences. A working prototype (dementia-coach.vercel.app) already demonstrates the core capability: given a caregiving scenario and a person-history profile, the system returns three labeled, technique-grounded response options in under fifteen seconds. Structured validation testing against the live endpoint — a 30-scenario labeled test set, a 40-case adversarial "Smart 40" stress log, and four fake-directive/prompt-injection safety tests — establishes TRL 3–4 for this component today, with 100% technique-category correctness and no reality-orientation slips across all 30 baseline scenarios. That same testing also surfaced real, unresolved gaps: intermittent parsing failures under minimal/ambiguous input, and one confirmed hallucinated biographical detail under adversarial probing. Phase I is designed to close exactly these gaps and build the components the current prototype does not yet cover — technique-effectiveness learning, stage-progression tracking, and distress escalation — while generating the outcome evidence this application category currently lacks.

**The long-term goal of this project is a validated, clinically-governed AI coaching tool that measurably reduces caregiver burden and behavioral-crisis frequency for family dementia caregivers.** The objective of this Phase I project is to establish technical and clinical feasibility: harden the coaching engine against the failure modes our own adversarial testing identified, build the stage-progression and effectiveness-learning components from design to working prototype, and generate the first controlled evidence — beyond adversarial technical testing — that personalized validation-therapy coaching changes caregiver behavior and reduces distress in a real caregiving population. We will pursue the following Specific Aims:

**Aim 1: Harden and clinically govern the core coaching engine.** Resolve the intermittent parsing/robustness failures and the hallucination pathway identified in our existing Smart-40 adversarial log; establish a clinical editorial board of validation-therapy-trained gerontological social workers to review AI-generated scripts against a structured accuracy rubric; expand the adversarial test suite from 40 to a larger, clinician-reviewed case set covering additional dementia stages and presentations.

**Aim 2: Build the stage-progression tracking and technique-effectiveness learning components from design (TRL 3) to working prototype (TRL 4–5).** Implement structured symptom/progression reporting with LLM-based stage inference, validated against clinical staging criteria by the editorial board; implement the caregiver feedback loop that lets the system learn which technique categories are most effective for a given care recipient over time; bench-test both components against clinician-authored ground truth.

**Aim 3: Establish preliminary evidence of caregiver-level impact through a small controlled pilot.** Recruit a pilot cohort of family dementia caregivers (target n=15–20) for an 8-week pre/post evaluation using the Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI-22) and the Revised Scale for Caregiving Self-Efficacy, alongside self-reported behavioral-crisis frequency and script-adoption rate; use results to power a Phase II randomized evaluation and to generate the first outcome evidence — beyond technical validation — for AI-delivered, personalized validation-therapy coaching at scale.

Successful completion of Phase I will establish Dementia Coach at TRL 5, produce the first controlled outcome data for this specific intervention category, and position the project for a Phase II randomized trial and clinical/telehealth integration partnerships. The innovation is not validation therapy itself, which is clinically established, but the mechanism to deliver it — personalized to a specific person's history, available in the moment a crisis is unfolding, at a scale no advisor-mediated or session-based model can reach.

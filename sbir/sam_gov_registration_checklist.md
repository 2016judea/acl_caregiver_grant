# SAM.gov / eRA Commons Registration Checklist

Prerequisite for NIA SBIR (PA-27-100) submission. Targeting the **January 5, 2027** receipt date — this has its own multi-week clock, so start now regardless.

## Real risk specific to your situation

**Brick and Mortar AI LLC's EIN was issued 2026-07-24 — six days old as of this writing.** SAM.gov validates your entity by cross-matching the IRS, the Secretary of State, and its own Federal Service Desk records. A brand-new EIN sometimes hasn't fully propagated through the IRS's matching database yet, which is a common cause of validation delay/rejection for very recently formed entities — on top of the normal 4–6 week first-time-registrant timeline. If SAM.gov rejects the initial attempt on a tax-ID mismatch, that's very likely why, not an error on your part — just retry after another week or two rather than assuming something's wrong with the LLC itself.

## Step 1 — Login.gov account

Create one if you don't already have one. Use a monitored business email, not a throwaway — this account gates SAM.gov, Grants.gov, and eRA Commons.

## Step 2 — Gather what SAM.gov will ask for

Already confirmed/available:
- **Legal entity name (exact IRS match):** BRICK AND MORTAR AI LLC
- **EIN:** [EIN]
- **Physical address (no PO box):** [address]

Still needed from you:
- **Bank routing + account number for EFT.** Does Brick and Mortar AI LLC have its own business bank account yet? If not, that's a prerequisite step before SAM.gov, not something to sort out mid-registration — flag this now if it doesn't exist.
- **NAICS code.** No single obviously-correct code for an AI/software R&D grant applicant — recommend **541511 (Custom Computer Programming Services)** as primary, since that's the standard fit for software-building SBIR grantees. If you want the same SAM.gov registration to also cover bricks' consulting work, consider adding **541690 (Other Scientific and Technical Consulting Services)** as a secondary code — SAM.gov allows multiple NAICS codes under one registration.
- **Registration purpose:** select "Federal Assistance" (grants), not "Federal Contracts" — SBIR is a grant mechanism (R43), not a procurement contract.

## Step 3 — Notarized Letter of Authorization: likely NOT needed

This is only required when the person registering isn't an officer of the entity. Since you're the sole member and you'd be registering yourself, this shouldn't apply — one real hurdle you get to skip.

## Step 4 — Submit and wait for UEI assignment

Your UEI (12-character alphanumeric) is auto-generated on successful validation. This is the identifier that follows you through Grants.gov and eRA Commons — nothing downstream can start without it.

## Step 5 — eRA Commons registration (after UEI, can start prep in parallel)

Needs the UEI to complete, but you can begin the organizational registration process once SAM.gov is submitted, not necessarily fully validated. Separate account types needed: organizational (Signing Official) and PI (you) — for a sole-member LLC, you'll likely hold both roles.

## Step 6 — Grants.gov AOR designation

Follows SAM.gov completion. For a sole-member LLC, the Authorized Organization Representative is you.

---

**Bottom line on sequencing:** none of steps 1–4 depend on anything else in the SBIR application — Aims, budget, Human Subjects, the Kris Foss conversation. Start this in parallel with everything else, not after.
